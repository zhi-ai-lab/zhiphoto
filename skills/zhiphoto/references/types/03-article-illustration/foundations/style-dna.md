# Style DNA

## One-line Direction

Pure white, minimal, hand-drawn, spacious, restrained, slightly bizarre, product-sketch-like, naturally handwritten, structurally clear without becoming an instruction sheet.

It should feel like a smart explanation sketch someone working in AI, product, design, or developer tools drew quickly on clean paper.

## Required

- 16:9 horizontal article illustration.
- Pure white background. No beige, warm gray, paper texture, gradients, shadows, noise, or vintage paper effects.
- Primarily hand-drawn line art with thin slightly wobbly lines. Do not make it mechanical, vector-clean, or thickly outlined.
- Generous empty space. Keep the main subject around 40%-60% of the canvas and leave at least 35% quiet blank area.
- Sparse handwritten annotations only when they help. Use the customer's requested language and keep each note short.
- One image expresses one core action, structure, state, or metaphor.
- The recurring character from the personal-IP template must feel naturally embedded in the structure, not pasted onto it.
- When a customer template is provided, preserve that character's illustration language/style and signature palette. Keep the sparse white hand-drawn article layout, but do not recolor the character to black unless the template itself is black.
- The black solid-character rule applies only to the Xiaohei fallback.
- Let the structure read visually. Do not write the structure type name on the artwork.

## Color

- Black for the main line art, structure, frames, and primary text. Use the recurring character's own signature palette when a customer template is provided; use black for the character only in the Xiaohei fallback branch.
- Red for key notes, problems, warnings, or outcomes.
- Orange for the main path, flow, or arrows.
- Blue for secondary notes, mental state, system state, feedback, or assistant/automation hints.

Blue is optional. Keep the palette restrained.

## Avoid

- Commercial illustration.
- PPT infographic style.
- Formal flowchart style.
- Course slide aesthetics.
- Cute mascot poster energy.
- Children's illustration.
- Dense architecture diagrams.
- Polished flat-design illustration.
- Futuristic UI screens.
- Real app screenshots.
- Complex backgrounds, gradients, shadows, or textures.
- Over-explaining every node.
- Top-left titles such as `Workflow`, `Architecture`, `Common Pitfalls`, or `Roadmap`.

## Aesthetic Direction

Aim for weird, inventive, clean, fresh, and memorable.

Avoid cute, childish, crowded, or rigid results.
