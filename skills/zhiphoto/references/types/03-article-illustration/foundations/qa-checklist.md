# QA Checklist

## Must pass

- 16:9 horizontal output.
- Clean white background.
- The recurring character is present, either from the customer's template or the Xiaohei fallback.
- The recurring character performs the core action instead of decorating the scene.
- When a customer template is used, the recurring character keeps its identity, illustration language/style, and signature palette. It is not flattened into a generic black Xiaohei-like silhouette.
- The image uses a fresh metaphor for the current article rather than recreating an old case.
- The result feels strange, inventive, and memorable.
- The composition stays clean and spacious, with the main subject under about 60% of the canvas.
- One image expresses one core structure.
- Labels are sparse, short, readable, and follow the customer's requested language when labels are present.
- Orange appears only in the main path or arrows.
- Red appears only for key points, problems, warnings, or outcomes.
- Blue appears only for secondary notes, feedback, or system state.

## Failure signals

Regenerate or edit when any of these appear:

- A top-left title such as `Common Pitfalls`, `Workflow`, `Architecture`, or `Roadmap`.
- The recurring character looks like a mascot, emoji, or cute cartoon.
- A customer-provided recurring character was recolored into a generic black figure or lost its signature palette/style.
- The image looks like a PPT slide, courseware, or formal flowchart.
- Too many elements, arrows, or nodes.
- Labels turn into long explanations.
- The background has paper texture, shadow, gradient, beige tint, or noise.
- Real UI screenshots or futuristic interfaces appear.
- Labels are badly misspelled or unreadable.
- The scene is rigid and lacks an absurd metaphor.
- The composition is too close to a prior example.

## Iteration levers

- Too ordinary: make the recurring character own the action and add one strange but coherent metaphor.
- Customer IP lost: restore the template's identity anchors, illustration language/style, and signature palette while keeping the article-layout scene sparse and hand-drawn.
- Too complex: remove nodes and keep only one action plus 3-5 short labels.
- Too cute: restate `deadpan`, `blank serious expression`, `not cute`, and `not mascot`.
- Too PPT-like: remove titles, borders, tidy grids, and excessive arrows; switch back to a hand-drawn scene.
- Too close to an old example: keep the core meaning, but change the main object and the character's action.
- Text errors: prefer local edits first; if errors are widespread, regenerate with fewer labels.

## Delivery bar

A strong result should feel slightly weird at first glance and then become legible within about one second.

If it reads like a tutorial page instead of an absurd product sketch on white paper, it is not ready.
