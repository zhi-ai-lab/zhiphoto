# Human Photo Realism Foundation

Use these shared decisions for every realistic-human-photo profile unless the user explicitly chooses a polished or stylized result.

## Skin truth

- Render naturally uneven skin texture: visible pores, fine vellus hair, and micro-variation in tone across the face.
- Include believable localized coloration, such as faint redness around the nostrils and mild under-eye shading, as ordinary human detail rather than flaws.
- Preserve real subsurface translucency so light appears to pass slightly into the skin instead of bouncing off a painted surface.
- State explicitly that there is no skin smoothing, no beauty-filter effect, and no retouching; the finish is documentary-realistic.

## Human structure

- Keep natural facial asymmetry and credible anatomy: eyes, teeth, ears, hairline, and any visible hands must withstand close inspection.
- Keep eyes lively and dimensional, with catchlights consistent with the stated light source; avoid glassy doll eyes.
- Let hair include flyaways and real strand behavior rather than a sculpted mass.

## Camera and light behavior

- Describe one motivated key-light direction and keep highlights, shadow softness, skin sheen, and background exposure consistent with it.
- Use true-to-life natural color and cinematic but natural contrast; never HDR glow, crushed cinematic teal-orange, or lifted matte haze unless the user asks.
- Render authentic lens behavior for the stated focal length: real depth-of-field falloff, focus placed deliberately, and photographic grain-level cleanliness rather than synthetic perfection.
- The result must read as a photograph, not a CGI render: no 3D-render sheen, waxy or plastic skin, or perfect bilateral symmetry.

## Shared avoidances

- Beauty-app smoothing, airbrushed gradients, or poreless skin.
- Over-sharpened texture that turns pores into noise.
- Studio-catalog blandness replacing the character the user described.
- Logos, signs, or readable text unless the user requests them.
