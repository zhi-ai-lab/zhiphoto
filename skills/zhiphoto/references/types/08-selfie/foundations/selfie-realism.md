# Selfie Realism Foundation

Use these shared decisions unless the user explicitly chooses a stylized or polished result.

## Capture geometry

- Make the image read as a phone held at arm's length: close perspective, plausible forearm or shoulder relationship, and mild wide-angle facial perspective without distortion.
- Prefer an imperfect crop, small camera tilt, and slightly off-center framing over a formal portrait composition.
- Keep the gaze, phone position, pose, reflections, and visible hands physically consistent. Do not add a visible phone unless the requested viewpoint calls for a mirror selfie.

## Human detail

- Preserve natural facial asymmetry, believable skin texture, flyaway hair, fabric behavior, and scene-appropriate grooming.
- Keep eyes aligned and expressive; treat squint, blink, wind, movement, or directional light as causal details rather than flaws.
- Require credible anatomy, especially hands, fingers, teeth, ears, hairlines, and the arm-to-camera relationship.

## Light and camera behavior

- State a motivated key-light direction and let it affect highlights, shadow softness, eye openness, skin sheen, and background exposure consistently.
- Allow restrained phone-camera artifacts when helpful: slight highlight clipping, shadow noise, minor motion softness, mixed white balance, and ordinary depth of field.
- Avoid synthetic beauty-filter skin, excessive HDR, perfect bilateral symmetry, plastic texture, and studio sharpness unless the chosen scene calls for them.

## Place and moment

- Use a few recognizable, spatially plausible cues instead of overloading the background with labels or landmarks.
- Match wardrobe, weather, time of day, crowd level, and body language to the setting.
- Keep logos, signs, and readable text out unless the user requests them and their accuracy matters.
