# Car-Interior Flash Realism Foundation

Use these shared decisions for every candid-car-flash-photo profile unless the user explicitly chooses a polished, stylized, or differently lit result.

## Capture logic

- Make the viewpoint physically plausible for a friend sitting nearby in the vehicle: a close phone-camera perspective from the cabin, not an impossible floating camera and not selfie geometry unless requested.
- Treat direct phone flash as a small, near-axis source. Keep the face and any nearby hands or props brighter than the cabin, with rapid falloff into deep but readable shadow.
- Keep highlights and shadows motivated by the same flash: specular points on skin, glass, metal, and cans can clip or look hard, while distant seats and the window side remain dim.
- Preserve casual occlusions and imperfect framing, such as a soft foreground seat belt, headrest edge, door frame, or cropped shoulder, when they support the requested intimacy.
- Place focus deliberately on the requested subject or gesture. Let near foreground and the outside view fall out of focus instead of making every plane equally sharp.

## Mixed night light

- Combine the hard white flash with any user-requested cool phone-screen glow, dashboard light, or other practical source, and keep each color source localized.
- Let street lamps, shop signs, or passing traffic outside the window form soft warm orange or amber bokeh when the brief calls for it; do not flood the entire image with a uniform teal-orange grade.
- Keep exposure intentionally low when requested, commonly around -1 to -2 EV as a photographic cue, while retaining small amounts of texture in the dark cabin.
- Allow restrained lens flare, halation, haze, or soft edge bloom that could come from a dirty phone lens, windshield moisture, or atmospheric smearing. These are accents, not a full-screen filter.

## Human and material truth

- Preserve natural skin texture, pores, fine facial hair, asymmetry, flyaways, tired eyes, and believable hands. State or imply no beauty filter, no poreless smoothing, and no plastic retouching.
- Make the expression and posture feel interrupted rather than posed: a glance toward the camera, a half-finished gesture, an unguarded mouth, or quiet fatigue may read more truthfully than a clear smile.
- Keep the seat upholstery, window reflections, safety belt, dashboard, drink containers, jewelry, and clothing folds consistent with the stated light and camera distance.
- Carry through the user's specified demographics, wardrobe, hair, props, and expression. If those are unspecified, choose neutral details that serve the moment without turning them into a type requirement.

## Shared avoidances

- Even multi-source illumination, ring-light skin, studio polish, beauty-app smoothing, airbrushed gradients, or a clean catalog pose.
- A bright fully visible car interior, an empty black background with no cabin cues, or a wide establishing shot that loses the private-space feeling unless requested.
- Overdone cyberpunk neon, crushed blacks with no retained detail, artificial HDR, excessive lens effects, or a poster-like cinematic still.
- Invented logos, readable labels, celebrity likenesses, or demographic and wardrobe defaults that the user did not request.
