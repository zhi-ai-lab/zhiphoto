# Late-Night Roadside Candid

## Use When

The request centers on a close, spontaneous moment inside a parked or briefly stopped car at night: someone has just been called, noticed, or interrupted and turns toward the nearby camera.

## Profile Deltas

- Build a compact composition in which the subject fills most of the frame, roughly two-thirds when no framing is specified, while a belt, seat edge, headrest, or door frame can create a soft foreground obstruction.
- Use a natural torso or shoulder turn toward the camera rather than a posed front-facing portrait. Preserve any specified hand action, prop, gaze, or clothing; when the brief leaves them open, choose one small unfinished gesture instead of adding a complete performance.
- Keep the face and nearest hands readable under hard white flash, while the cabin falls into dark, textured shadow. Add cool screen or dashboard spill and warm orange streetlight bokeh outside the window only when present in the brief or useful to explain the requested mood.
- Favor an unguarded expression—distracted, tired, mildly unfocused, or momentarily blank—when the prompt asks for an interrupted private moment. Do not replace a user-specified emotion with this default.
- Preserve a low-key blue-orange balance, modest grain, slight soft-focus at the edges, and an intimate sense of airless cabin space. Keep the result like a real phone snapshot from a friend, not a campaign image.

## Profile-Specific Avoidances

- Do not default to any nationality, ethnicity, gender, body type, hairstyle, age below adulthood, outfit, revealing garment, or sexualized pose.
- Do not automatically add beer, cigarettes, phones, or other lifestyle props when the user's brief does not mention them.
- Avoid a clean portrait-lens headshot, evenly exposed dashboard, perfect centered symmetry, cheerful posed smile, or dramatic action-movie lighting.
- Avoid turning the mixed practical light into a heavy teal-orange preset, making the bokeh look like a neon city, or erasing the car's physical enclosure.
